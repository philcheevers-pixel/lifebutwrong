Section A
The Invention
Molecular structure, mechanism, target, and supporting data.
Question 1
Describe the invention in plain language. What is it, and what does it do?
Non-technical summary first, then scientific detail. Think of explaining it to a patent examiner who doesn't work in your specific field.
→ Invention Classification, Claim Architecture Basis, Technology Domain
Question 2
What is the molecular structure? Provide sequences, formulas, or structural descriptions.
Biologics: amino acid sequences, CDR regions, variable regions. Small molecules: chemical formula, stereochemistry. Devices/processes: schematic or flowchart.
→ Structural Data, Sequence ID, Composition Claims Basis
Attach files (optional) — drag & drop or click to browse
SMILES strings, structure diagrams, patent PDFs, sequence files. Max 10 MB per file.
Question 3
What is the specific biological or chemical target? How does the invention interact with it?
Binding site, receptor, enzyme, pathway. What does it block, activate, modulate, or replace?
→ Target Identification, Mechanism Claims Basis, Design-Around Vulnerability Assessment
Question 4
What data do you have? Efficacy, safety, binding affinity, selectivity, stability.
In vitro data, animal studies, human data, analytical characterization. Note what's published vs. unpublished.
→ Data Maturity Assessment, Prior Art Exposure Risk, Enablement Support
Section B
Prior Art Landscape
What the inventor already knows. This is not exhaustive search — that's what the ABFTO report does.
Question 5
What existing products, patents, or publications are you aware of in this space?
Competitor products, published papers, known patent families, conference presentations. What's "close" to the invention?
→ Known Prior Art, Obviousness Risk Triage, Competitor Identification
Question 6
What makes your invention different from what already exists?
The inventive step in the scientist's own words. Better efficacy? Novel target? Different mechanism? New formulation?
→ Inventive Step Basis, Non-Obviousness Arguments, Claim Differentiation Strategy
This answer directly affects prior art analysis. Consider reviewing with supervising attorney before submission.
Question 7
Have you or your team published anything about this invention or related work?
Papers, posters, conference talks, abstracts, patents, investor presentations, grant applications. Include dates. Critical for 102/103 prior art exposure.
→ Self-Prior Art Exposure, Grace Period Calculation, Publication Bar Risk
This answer directly affects prior art analysis. Consider reviewing with supervising attorney before submission.
Section C
Commercial Intent & Strategic Context
Why the patent matters and how it will be used. Drives the strategic analysis.
Question 8
What are the target indications or therapeutic areas?
Primary indication, secondary indications, orphan drug potential. Include any regulatory designations (Fast Track, Breakthrough Therapy, etc.).
→ Indication Scope, Market Size Assessment, Regulatory Pathway Analysis
Question 9
What is the development stage and timeline?
Preclinical, Phase I/II/III, NDA/BLA filed, approved. Expected milestone dates. Partnerships affecting timeline.
→ Development Stage, Filing Priority Assessment, Competitive Window Analysis
Question 10
What are the target markets and jurisdictions?
US, EU, Japan, China, emerging markets. Where will you seek approval? Biggest revenue opportunities? Biggest patent threats?
→ Jurisdictional Topology, Filing Strategy, Regulatory Harmonization Gaps
Section D
Manufacturing & Formulation
Manufacturing patents are often the most defensible — and the most overlooked.
Question 11
Describe the production process.
Cell lines, expression systems, synthetic chemistry, purification steps, critical quality attributes. What's novel about the process?
→ Manufacturing Claims Basis, Process Patent Scope, Design-Around Barriers
Question 12
What is the formulation? Concentration, excipients, delivery system, stability profile.
Buffer system, surfactants, stabilizers, pH range, container closure (vial, syringe, autoinjector). Stability data if available.
→ Formulation Claims Basis, Delivery System Protection, Lifecycle Extension Opportunities
Question 13
Are there alternative manufacturing methods or formulations you've considered or tested?
What the inventor tried and rejected is as valuable as what works. Identifies competitor design-around pathways.
→ Design-Around Pathway Identification, Negative Data Value, Defensive Patent Opportunities
Section E
Existing IP & Legal Context
Current patents, applications, licensing. The IP landscape already around this invention.
Question 14
Do you have existing patents, applications, or provisional filings related to this invention?
Patent numbers, application numbers, filing dates, priority dates. Continuations, divisionals, CIPs. Foreign filings or PCT applications.
→ Existing Portfolio Mapping, Priority Date Chain, Continuation Strategy Assessment
Question 15
Are there any licensing agreements, collaborations, or joint development arrangements?
Co-inventors, institutional IP policies, collaboration agreements, MTAs, government grant conditions (Bayh-Dole march-in rights, etc.).
→ Ownership Assessment, Freedom to Operate Constraints, Licensing Landscape
Section F
Risk Awareness
The inventor's own concerns. Often surfaces risks formal analysis might miss.
Question 16
What keeps you up at night about protecting this invention?
Express concerns in your own words. Weak points, potential challenges, competitors that worry you most.
→ Inventor-Flagged Vulnerabilities, Subjective Risk Assessment, Investigation Priorities
